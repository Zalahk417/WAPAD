Sources / verification

Sources,
not slogans.

WAPAD’s scope boundaries, compliance framing and testing guidance are linked to regulators and recognised professional bodies. The digital desk can travel; regulated dealing and professional credentials do not magically travel with it.

01VALUER CREDENTIALS

National Council of Jewellery Valuers

NCJV membership provides the clearest Australian credibility boundary for formal jewellery valuation. WAPAD’s model routes formal valuation work to appropriately credentialled professionals instead of labelling digital market research a registered valuation.

Professional bodyOpen NCJV membership standards ↗
02MEASUREMENT

National Measurement Institute

Trade measurement requirements relevant to buying, selling and valuing precious metals and stones by measurement, including approved/verified scales, verification intervals and customer visibility of weighing.

Primary sourceOpen NMI guidance ↗
03WA LICENSING

WA Police — Second-Hand Dealer licensing

Western Australian rules for businesses buying, selling or exchanging second-hand goods. WAPAD’s controlled digital pilot deliberately avoids making custody and second-hand dealing the core product.

Primary sourceOpen WA guidance ↗
04INTERSTATE

State rules change when the operator moves

Second-hand dealing is separately regulated across Australian jurisdictions. NSW and Victoria, for example, operate their own licensing or registration systems. That is one reason the portable WAPAD core is decision support and specialist routing rather than inventory acquisition.

05TESTING

Gemological Institute of America

Bench guidance on the touchstone method for determining karat-gold purity and its practical testing context. WAPAD uses testing references to form an escalation plan, not to imply a browser can replace physical examination.

Specialist sourceOpen GIA guidance ↗
06AML / CTF

AUSTRAC — Precious metals, stones and products

Current guidance for newly regulated dealers, including designated-service boundaries, linked transactions and payment-method context. It remains a launch-gate source if WAPAD later changes from decision support into relevant transactions.

Primary sourceOpen AUSTRAC guidance ↗
07WA LAW

Pawnbrokers and Second-hand Dealers Act 1994 (WA)

Primary legislation covering licensing, records and retention. This is relevant to any future WA dealing model, but not a reason to make asset ownership the centre of the digital desk.

Primary sourceOpen WA legislation ↗
08ASSET MANIFEST

Legacy image source manifest

Earlier WAPAD design drafts used selected Wikimedia Commons imagery. The current interface is deliberately self-contained and does not depend on those third-party hero/gallery images, but the existing manifest remains available for provenance and licence records.

Useful before
you change the asset.

Short guides designed around one rule: preserve optionality until you know what evidence matters.

GOLD6 MIN

Reading hallmarks without over-reading them

A hallmark or fineness mark is evidence, not a complete authentication. “750”, “18K” or a maker’s stamp can guide the next test, but marks can be worn, misleading, applied to only part of an object, or simply wrong.

Record before testing

  • Photograph the mark in context and close-up.
  • Record every mark, not only the one that looks like purity.
  • Note whether different parts carry different marks.
  • Do not file, scratch or acid-test a potentially collectable surface simply to get an answer quickly.
Desk ruleUse the mark to form a test plan, not to skip the test plan.
WATCH7 MIN

Before opening, polishing or servicing a watch

On some watches, originality and condition can be worth more than cosmetic perfection. A polished case, replaced dial, discarded bracelet or missing service part can permanently change the collectable route.

Prepare first

  • Photograph dial, case, crown, bracelet, clasp, case-back and any visible reference/serial.
  • Keep box, papers, tags, spare links and service receipts together.
  • Do not force a crown or case-back.
  • Record whether the watch runs, but do not keep winding an obviously distressed movement.
Desk ruleDocument originality before “improving” condition.
BULLION5 MIN

Why weight alone does not authenticate bullion

A correct gross weight is useful, but a counterfeit can be engineered around weight. A sensible screen layers dimensions, density or specific-gravity context, magnetic behaviour, surface/edge detail, packaging and known mint specifications.

Useful evidence

  • Exact item type, mint and stated weight/fineness.
  • Dimensions measured with suitable equipment.
  • Serial number or packaging details where applicable.
  • High-resolution edge and surface photographs.
Desk ruleOne passing test should not erase several conflicting signals.
ESTATE8 MIN

Estate parcel triage before anything is split

The first job is not valuation. It is preserving relationships between objects, papers and provenance long enough to decide what deserves deeper work.

First pass

  • Photograph the parcel as found.
  • Keep matched pieces, boxes and papers together.
  • Separate obvious precious-metal marks without discarding unmarked pieces.
  • Flag signed jewellery, watches, coins, unusual stones and items with family documentation.
  • Do not bulk-clean the parcel.
Desk ruleTriage creates options; premature sorting can destroy them.
VALUE6 MIN

The metal floor is important — and incomplete

Recoverable precious-metal value is a useful floor for many objects. It is not automatically the correct sale route. A finished object can carry value from design, maker, stones, condition, rarity, provenance or demand.

Separate the questions

  • What recoverable precious material is actually present?
  • What are realistic downstream deductions?
  • Is there a market for the intact item?
  • Would destructive testing or refining remove a premium that cannot be recovered?
Desk ruleCalculate the floor without assuming the floor is the ceiling.
FIRST RULE4 MIN

Preserve before you improve

Cleaning, polishing, resizing, separating stones, opening cases, removing labels or throwing away damaged packaging can make an item easier to handle and harder to understand.

Safe default

  • Dry, stable storage.
  • Separate soft wraps for pieces that can scratch each other.
  • No chemical cleaning until material and construction are understood.
  • Keep every document, tag, box, link, broken component and receipt with the object.
Desk ruleReversible actions first. Irreversible actions only with a reason.
